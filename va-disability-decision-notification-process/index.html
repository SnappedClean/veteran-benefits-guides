VA Disability Decision Process: Rating, Decision Letter, Notification and Review Options

A VA disability claim can feel opaque because the portal shows short status labels while a lot is happening behind the scenes. The key stages near the end of a claim are evidence review, rating, preparation of the decision letter, final review, and claim decided. Understanding what each stage means can help veterans avoid reading too much into a temporary status change.

Quick answer: VA's current process shows evidence review, rating, preparing the decision letter, final review, and claim decided as later claim stages. A claim can move backward if VA needs more evidence. When the claim is decided, VA makes the decision letter available in the claim-status tool and also mails a copy.

Evidence Review: VA Looks at the Entire Record

Before VA can rate a disability claim, it reviews the evidence in the file. That may include service treatment records, personnel records, VA medical records, private medical evidence, lay statements, C&P examination reports, medical opinions, and records submitted after the claim was filed.

A portal status of “evidence review” does not mean no more evidence can be requested. VA's own process page states that if more evidence is needed—or if more evidence is submitted—the claim can return to evidence gathering. That back-and-forth is normal and does not by itself predict approval or denial.

What Happens During the VA Rating Stage?

At the rating stage, VA decides the claim and determines the disability rating for conditions that are service connected. The rater applies law, regulation, the VA Schedule for Rating Disabilities, and the evidence in the file. A claim with several issues can produce a mix of grants, denials, deferred issues, and different effective dates.

The rating stage is not simply “pick a percentage.” For each claimed issue, VA may need to answer whether service connection is established, the correct diagnostic criteria, the severity shown by the evidence, and the proper effective date.

What Does “Preparing Decision Letter” Mean?

VA says this stage means it is preparing the written decision letter. If the veteran is eligible for disability benefits, the letter can include the disability rating, the amount of monthly payments, and the date payments begin. A portal label showing this stage does not tell you whether the outcome is favorable.

Internet forums often treat “preparing decision letter” or similar status text as a hidden signal about the result. It is safer to wait for the actual decision letter. A claim can still go back to evidence gathering if something needs correction or more evidence is required.

Final Review and Claim Decided

VA's published process states that a senior reviewer performs a final review of the claim and decision letter. Once the claim is marked decided, the veteran can review and download the decision letter in the online claim-status tool. VA also mails a copy and says the mailed letter should generally arrive within 10 business days, although it can take longer.

Save the PDF as soon as it is available. The decision letter is far more important than the status history because it explains exactly what VA decided and why.

How to Read a VA Disability Decision Letter

Do not stop at the combined rating shown near the top. Read each issue separately. Look for:

  • Which conditions were granted, denied, or deferred
  • The percentage assigned to each granted condition
  • The effective date for each award
  • The evidence VA says it considered
  • The reasons for the decision
  • Any favorable findings
  • Information about review rights and deadlines

Effective dates can be just as financially important as percentages. If the percentage is correct but the effective date is wrong, the veteran may still have a significant review issue.

Why the Combined VA Rating May Not Equal Simple Addition

When a veteran has multiple ratings, VA uses its combined-ratings method rather than ordinary addition. Two 50% ratings do not simply become 100%. This is why a decision letter can show several substantial individual percentages without a 100% combined rating.

Separate rules such as the bilateral factor can also affect the combined evaluation in qualifying cases. Use VA's official combined-ratings information rather than relying on mental arithmetic.

What If You Disagree With the VA Decision?

For most decisions in the modern review system, veterans generally have three main review options. The correct lane depends on why the decision is wrong.

  • Supplemental Claim: Use this when you have new and relevant evidence.
  • Higher-Level Review: Ask a more senior reviewer to examine the existing record for error. New evidence is not submitted in this lane.
  • Board Appeal: Appeal to a Veterans Law Judge, choosing among available Board dockets with different evidence/hearing rules.

VA generally requires Higher-Level Review and Board Appeal requests within one year of the decision notice for eligible decisions. Supplemental Claims may be filed later, but timing can affect the potential effective date. Read the review instructions in your own decision letter carefully.

Common Errors to Check in a Decision

Potential issues can include VA overlooking relevant records, relying on an inadequate medical opinion, using the wrong rating criteria, assigning an incorrect effective date, misunderstanding a secondary-service-connection theory, failing to address favorable evidence, or deciding a claim before requested evidence was obtained. Not every disagreement is an error, but the written reasons help you identify the exact issue.

If the claim is complex, consider a VA-accredited VSO representative, claims agent, or attorney. VA maintains an accreditation search so veterans can verify that a representative is authorized.

What Happens After a VA Disability Grant?

If VA grants compensation, payment timing depends on the effective date, payment-start rules, and whether retroactive benefits are due. The decision letter should identify the rating and effective-date information. Veterans who become eligible for additional benefits because of the new rating should separately check health care, dependent compensation, dental eligibility, home-loan fee exemption, and other programs instead of assuming everything enrolls automatically.

VA Disability Decision FAQ

Does “rating” mean my VA claim is almost done?

It is a later claim stage, but VA can return the claim to evidence gathering if more information is needed. It is not a guarantee of an immediate decision.

Does “preparing decision letter” mean my claim was approved?

No. That status only tells you VA is preparing the written decision. Wait for the actual decision letter.

Where can I download my VA decision letter?

VA says the letter becomes available in the claim-status tool when the claim is decided. A mailed copy is also sent.

How long do I have to challenge a VA decision?

Deadlines depend on the review lane. Higher-Level Review and Board Appeal requests generally have a one-year deadline for eligible modern decisions. Supplemental Claims have different timing rules, and filing within one year can matter for effective dates.
