Top Agent Orange Disability Claims: 5 Common Presumptive Conditions Veterans Should Know

VA's Agent Orange presumptive list is much longer than five conditions. This guide focuses on five conditions veterans frequently search for—hypertension, Type 2 diabetes, ischemic heart disease, prostate cancer, and Parkinson's disease/Parkinsonism—and explains the basic claim logic behind each.

Important: These are five widely searched examples—not an official VA ranking of the “top” claims. VA recognizes many additional Agent Orange presumptive diseases. A presumptive claim still requires evidence of a current diagnosis and qualifying service or exposure.

How Agent Orange Presumptive Claims Work

When VA recognizes both a veteran's qualifying herbicide exposure and a diagnosed condition as presumptive, the veteran generally does not have to prove the same individual medical nexus that would usually be needed to connect the illness to service. That can simplify a claim significantly.

The remaining questions still matter: Was the veteran in a recognized location during the required period? Is there a current diagnosis? How severe is the disability? What is the correct effective date? Are there secondary conditions caused or aggravated by the presumptive disability?

1. Hypertension and Agent Orange

High blood pressure, or hypertension, became an Agent Orange presumptive condition under the PACT Act. This was a major change because hypertension is common among aging Vietnam-era veterans and had historically required a different evidentiary path.

A veteran pursuing this claim should make sure the medical record clearly documents the diagnosis and treatment. Blood-pressure readings, medication records, primary-care notes, and cardiology records can all be relevant to how VA evaluates the condition. The fact that hypertension is presumptive does not mean every veteran receives the same percentage; the rating depends on VA's applicable criteria and evidence.

2. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

Type 2 diabetes is a long-standing Agent Orange presumptive condition. In addition to the diabetes rating itself, veterans should understand that diabetes can produce complications affecting other body systems. Depending on the facts, complications may be evaluated separately or as part of the diabetic process under VA rules.

Useful evidence can include A1C history, medication or insulin treatment, dietary restrictions, physician notes, hospital records, and documentation of complications such as peripheral neuropathy, kidney disease, vision problems, or other conditions. Whether a complication qualifies for separate service connection or rating is an individual adjudication issue.

3. Ischemic Heart Disease

VA's Agent Orange presumptive list includes ischemic heart disease. The term can cover certain coronary conditions associated with reduced blood supply to the heart, but not every heart diagnosis is automatically the same thing. Veterans should identify the exact diagnosis from cardiology records rather than simply filing for “heart problems.”

Records such as stress tests, catheterization reports, coronary artery disease diagnoses, stent or bypass history, ejection-fraction studies, medication lists, and documented functional limitations can be important. VA rating questions may involve symptoms and objective testing in addition to the diagnosis itself.

4. Prostate Cancer

Prostate cancer is a presumptive cancer associated with Agent Orange exposure. Cancer claims can work differently from chronic-condition claims because VA rating rules can distinguish active malignancy and treatment from residual effects after treatment ends.

Pathology reports, biopsy results, oncology notes, radiation or surgery records, and follow-up documentation are especially important. Veterans who have completed cancer treatment should still document residual problems, because urinary, sexual, surgical, or other residuals may affect the long-term evaluation after active treatment.

5. Parkinson's Disease and Parkinsonism

VA recognizes Parkinson's disease and Parkinsonism among Agent Orange presumptive conditions. Neurological claims can involve multiple symptoms, including tremor, slowed movement, balance problems, speech changes, cognitive effects, swallowing difficulty, and other functional limitations.

A neurologist's diagnosis, treatment notes, medication history, occupational or physical therapy records, and documentation of daily functional effects can help VA understand the full disability picture. Veterans should report all diagnosed manifestations rather than assuming one percentage captures every effect automatically.

Other Agent Orange Presumptive Conditions

The official VA list includes many additional diseases, including bladder cancer, chronic B-cell leukemias, Hodgkin's disease, multiple myeloma, non-Hodgkin's lymphoma, respiratory cancers including lung cancer, hypothyroidism, MGUS, and others. The PACT Act added MGUS along with hypertension and broadened qualifying exposure locations.

If your diagnosis is not one of the five highlighted here, that does not mean it is excluded. Use VA's current official list. And if a condition is not presumptive at all, a veteran may still pursue direct service connection when competent evidence supports a relationship to military service.

Filing Tips for Agent Orange Claims

  1. Claim the specific diagnosed condition rather than only a symptom.
  2. Make sure VA has service records showing qualifying dates and locations.
  3. Use current medical records to establish the diagnosis.
  4. Identify secondary complications when they are medically documented.
  5. Attend any C&P examinations VA schedules.
  6. Read the final decision's reasons and effective date carefully.
  7. If the decision is wrong, choose the review lane that matches the problem: new evidence, an error in the existing record, or a Board appeal.
Search tip: “Top Agent Orange claims” is a useful way to find information, but VA does not publish an official popularity ranking. Eligibility is based on your service, diagnosis, and evidence—not how common a claim is.

Common Agent Orange Claim FAQ

Is high blood pressure now presumptive for Agent Orange?

Yes. Hypertension was added under the PACT Act.

Is Type 2 diabetes presumptive?

Yes. Type 2 diabetes mellitus is on VA's Agent Orange presumptive-condition list.

Is prostate cancer presumptive?

Yes. Prostate cancer is included among VA's Agent Orange presumptive cancers.

What if I have lung cancer?

Respiratory cancers, including lung cancer, appear on VA's Agent Orange presumptive list. Review the current VA page and your qualifying service history.
