5 Unconventional Ways to Use Your GI Bill Beyond a Traditional College Degree

The GI Bill is often marketed as a college benefit, but VA education benefits can support many other career paths. Depending on your eligibility and the approved program, benefits may help with aviation, apprenticeships, licensing tests, trade programs, online learning, and other job-focused training.

Quick answer: VA's own GI Bill guidance lists options beyond a traditional degree, including flight training, non-college degree programs, licensing and certification tests, on-the-job training, apprenticeships, online learning, and certain entrepreneurship or high-tech career training.

1. Use the GI Bill for Approved Flight Training

Veterans interested in aviation may be able to use the Post-9/11 GI Bill or certain Montgomery GI Bill programs for approved flight training. The rules differ depending on whether the flight training is part of a college degree program or a standalone pilot school or training center.

Standalone training generally requires a private pilot license, an appropriate FAA medical certificate, an FAA Part 141 school or Part 142 training center, and a program approved for VA education benefits. If your long-term goal is commercial aviation, compare a university aviation degree with standalone certificate training before deciding how to spend entitlement.

2. Get GI Bill Benefits During an Apprenticeship or On-the-Job Training

One of the least understood GI Bill options is the ability to receive VA education payments while earning wages in an approved apprenticeship or on-the-job training program. VA gives examples such as plumbing, electrical work, law enforcement, and firefighting.

In a qualifying program, the employer pays your salary while VA may provide a monthly benefit as you train. For Post-9/11 GI Bill users, books and supplies may also be covered. The monthly payment typically decreases as you progress through the training because your employer wages are expected to increase.

3. Get Reimbursed for Licensing and Certification Tests

GI Bill benefits can reimburse the cost of approved licensing and certification tests. That can be useful for veterans who already have the knowledge or training needed for a career but need a recognized credential to enter or advance in the field.

Depending on approval, this can include professional licensing exams, certification tests, and certain preparatory courses. The important point is that VA pays for approved tests under specific rules; it does not mean every private credential or bootcamp qualifies.

4. Use the GI Bill for a Non-College Degree or Trade Program

Approved non-college degree programs can be a strong option for veterans who want a direct path into a trade or technical career. VA lists examples such as HVAC repair, truck driving, EMT training, and other vocational programs. These programs may include tuition payments and, depending on the GI Bill program, housing and books.

Before enrolling, use the GI Bill Comparison Tool to verify the school and exact program. A school advertising “veteran friendly” or “GI Bill accepted” is not the same as proof that the particular course you want is approved.

5. Use Benefits for Online Learning, High-Tech Training, or Business Skills

VA education benefits may support approved online courses through institutions of higher learning. Post-9/11 GI Bill users taking only online courses can qualify for a housing allowance under a different rate structure than students taking qualifying in-person coursework.

VA's GI Bill guidance also points veterans toward certain high-tech programs and training designed to help start a business. These options change over time, so veterans should confirm the current program and benefit rules rather than relying on an old list.

How to Choose the Best GI Bill Use for You

The best use of the GI Bill is not always the option with the highest advertised tuition. Consider the career outcome, program completion rate, total months of entitlement used, housing allowance, out-of-pocket costs, licensing requirements, and whether the training leads to a credential employers actually value.

  1. Check your remaining entitlement and eligibility percentage.
  2. Define the job or career outcome you want.
  3. Search the exact program in the GI Bill Comparison Tool.
  4. Compare total costs, not just tuition.
  5. Ask how many months of entitlement the program will consume.
  6. Confirm whether housing, books, fees, exams, and supplies are included.
  7. Verify any required licenses or post-training exams.
SEO reality check: Searches like “weird things you can use the GI Bill for” often produce lists with outdated or oversimplified claims. VA approval is program-specific. Verify the current official rules before enrolling or paying a deposit.

Unconventional GI Bill Uses FAQ

Can I use the GI Bill for trade school?

Yes, approved non-college degree programs may qualify. VA lists examples such as HVAC repair, truck driving, and EMT training.

Can the GI Bill pay me during an apprenticeship?

Approved OJT and apprenticeship programs may provide VA monthly payments while you also earn wages from the employer.

Can the GI Bill pay for certification exams?

VA may reimburse approved licensing and certification tests and, in some cases, preparatory courses.

Can I use the GI Bill for online school?

Yes, approved online courses through institutions of higher learning may qualify, though housing allowance rules differ for online-only students.
