What Happens After a C&P Exam? VA Claim Steps and What to Do Next

Finishing a Compensation & Pension exam can feel like the biggest part of a VA disability claim, but the exam is usually one piece of the evidence. Afterward, VA may continue gathering evidence, review the file, assign ratings, prepare the decision letter, conduct final review, and then issue the decision.

Quick answer: After a C&P exam, the examiner's report is returned for use as evidence. VA may still gather records or order more exams. The claim can then move through evidence review, rating, preparation of the decision letter, final review, and “claim decided.”

What Happens to the C&P Exam Report?

The examiner does not normally hand you the final VA disability rating at the appointment. The purpose of the C&P exam is to collect medical evidence relevant to the claim. The examiner documents findings, history, symptoms, functional impact, and other information requested by VA. That report then becomes part of the evidence reviewed by the people deciding the claim.

A positive-feeling exam does not guarantee approval, and an uncomfortable exam does not automatically mean denial. VA evaluates the entire record, including service records, medical records, statements, prior examinations, and other evidence.

VA Claim Stages After the Exam

VA's current claim-process guidance describes stages that can include evidence gathering, evidence review, rating, preparation of a decision letter, final review, and a completed decision. Claims can also move backward if VA decides it needs more evidence.

  • Evidence gathering: VA may still request records, clarification, or another exam.
  • Evidence review: The evidence in the file is reviewed.
  • Rating: VA determines service connection and the appropriate evaluation for each issue.
  • Preparing decision letter: VA prepares the written explanation of the decision.
  • Final review: A senior reviewer performs a final check.
  • Claim decided: The decision letter becomes available and is also mailed.

What Should You Do After a C&P Exam?

  1. Write down what happened while it is fresh. Keep private notes about questions, tests, symptoms discussed, and anything important that occurred.
  2. Watch your VA claim status. Use VA.gov rather than relying on unofficial status interpretations.
  3. Respond to requests quickly. If VA asks for evidence, forms, or another exam, pay attention to the deadline.
  4. Keep your contact information current. Missing a letter or exam notice can create avoidable problems.
  5. Avoid assuming every status change predicts the outcome. Claims often move between stages.
  6. Save every decision document. The reasoning in the final letter matters if you later request review.

Can VA Order Another C&P Exam?

Yes. A second exam does not automatically mean something went wrong. VA may need another medical opinion, clarification, an exam for a different claimed condition, or updated information. The claim can return to evidence gathering even after it appeared to be moving toward a rating decision.

If VA schedules another examination, attend it unless VA or the contractor formally changes the appointment. Keep records of rescheduling communications.

What Is in the VA Decision Letter?

VA states that when the claim is decided, the decision letter can be reviewed and downloaded through the claim-status tool, and a copy is mailed. If benefits are granted, the letter can include the disability rating, payment amount, and effective-date information. The decision also explains why VA granted or denied each issue.

Read the written reasons carefully. The percentage alone does not tell you whether VA accepted the claimed service connection, what effective date was assigned, or why an issue was denied.

What If You Disagree With the Decision?

For most modern VA benefit decisions, the main review lanes are a Supplemental Claim, Higher-Level Review, or Board Appeal. A Supplemental Claim is used when you have new and relevant evidence. A Higher-Level Review asks a more senior reviewer to look for an error using the existing evidence. A Board Appeal sends the case to a Veterans Law Judge, with different docket options.

Deadlines matter. VA generally states that Higher-Level Review and Board Appeal requests must be submitted within one year of the decision for eligible claims. A Supplemental Claim can be filed later, but filing within one year can matter for preserving potential effective-date rights.

Useful mindset: The C&P exam is not the finish line. The decision letter—not the examiner's tone, a portal rumor, or a status label—is the document that tells you what VA actually decided.

After the C&P Exam FAQ

How long does a VA decision take after the C&P exam?

There is no fixed number of days. Timing depends on the number of issues, whether records are outstanding, whether another medical opinion is needed, and VA workload.

Can I see my C&P exam results?

Access depends on who performed the exam and where the report is stored. Veterans can request records through the appropriate VA records process. Do not assume the examiner can release the report directly at the appointment.

Does “preparing decision letter” mean I won?

No. It means VA is preparing the written decision. The result can be a grant, denial, partial grant, deferred issue, or combination.

What if my claim goes backward to evidence gathering?

That can happen if VA needs additional records, clarification, or another exam. It does not by itself predict the final outcome.
