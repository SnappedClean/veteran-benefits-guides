Can You Work With 100% VA Disability in 2026?

The answer depends on whether your compensation is based on a schedular 100% VA disability rating or Total Disability Based on Individual Unemployability (TDIU). Those are not the same thing.

Short answer: a schedular 100% VA rating is different from TDIU. TDIU is specifically based on service-connected disabilities preventing substantially gainful employment.

100% Schedular VA Disability and Working

A schedular 100% VA disability rating is reached through VA's disability rating system. It is not, by itself, an Individual Unemployability award. That distinction matters when veterans search for “can I work with 100 percent VA disability?” or “will working affect my VA disability?”

What Is TDIU?

TDIU stands for Total Disability Based on Individual Unemployability. VA may pay an eligible veteran at the 100% disability rate when service-connected disabilities prevent the veteran from maintaining substantially gainful employment, even when the schedular rating is below 100%.

Can You Work With 100% Permanent and Total (P&T) VA Disability?

Permanent and Total status and TDIU answer different questions. P&T concerns the permanence and total level of qualifying service-connected disability. TDIU concerns unemployability. Veterans should check their decision letter to determine the basis of their award.

What Does “Substantially Gainful Employment” Mean?

VA uses the concept of substantially gainful employment when evaluating Individual Unemployability. VA also distinguishes marginal employment from substantially gainful employment. If TDIU is involved, the details of employment can matter.
