100% VA Disability Benefits: What Disabled Veterans May Qualify For in 2026

Monthly compensation is only one part of a 100% VA disability rating. VA's current federal benefit matrix identifies health care, dental care, travel reimbursement, dependent compensation, home-loan advantages, federal employment benefits, commissary and exchange access, and other programs that may apply. Some family benefits require the rating to be Permanent and Total.

Quick answer: VA's current benefit matrix lists no-cost VA health care and prescriptions, travel allowance for qualifying VA care, no-cost dental care, VA home-loan funding-fee waiver, federal hiring preference, VR&E, additional dependent compensation, commissary/exchange/MWR privileges, and other benefits at the 100% service-connected level. CHAMPVA and Chapter 35 DEA generally require permanence.

First: Know What Kind of “100%” Rating You Have

Not every veteran paid at the 100% rate has the same status. A veteran can reach 100% through the regular rating schedule, receive Total Disability Based on Individual Unemployability (TDIU), have a temporary total rating, or have a 100% rating that VA also considers Permanent and Total. Those distinctions can change which benefits apply.

Before using a benefits checklist, look at your VA decision and benefit summary. Search for language showing whether the rating is Permanent and Total and whether Individual Unemployability is involved. This is especially important for family health and education programs.

No-Cost VA Health Care and Prescription Benefits

VA's benefit eligibility matrix lists no-cost health care and prescription medications at the 100% service-connected disability level. Specific enrollment and care rules still apply, but a total service-connected rating places the veteran in a strong priority category for VA health benefits.

VA health care is separate from private insurance. Veterans can still have Medicare, employer coverage, or other insurance while using VA care, subject to the rules of each program.

VA Dental Care for 100% Disabled Veterans

Dental care is one of the most valuable benefits veterans miss because ordinary VA medical enrollment does not automatically provide comprehensive dental treatment to everyone. VA's dental eligibility page places veterans with one or more service-connected disabilities rated 100% disabling in Class IV, which may qualify them for any needed dental care.

VA specifically notes that a temporary 100% rating for hospitalization or rehabilitation does not qualify by itself for this Class IV benefit. Veterans receiving compensation at the 100% rate because they are unemployable due to service-connected conditions are also listed under Class IV, subject to VA rules.

Travel Reimbursement for VA Care

VA's federal benefit matrix lists travel allowance for scheduled appointments at VA medical facilities or VA-authorized care at the 100% service-connected level. Actual travel reimbursement is administered under the beneficiary travel program, which has its own rules for mileage, qualifying trips, claims, and deadlines.

VA Home Loan Funding-Fee Exemption

VA generally exempts veterans receiving compensation for a service-connected disability from the VA home-loan funding fee. This is broader than only 100% ratings, but the savings can be substantial for a veteran using a VA-backed purchase or refinance loan.

The funding-fee exemption does not remove ordinary lender closing costs, prepaid taxes or insurance, interest, or other costs. It specifically removes the VA funding fee when the veteran meets VA's exemption requirements.

Additional Compensation for Dependents

Veterans with qualifying service-connected disability ratings may receive additional compensation for eligible dependents. At 100%, the difference between a veteran alone and a veteran with a spouse, children, or qualifying parents can be meaningful. Certain circumstances can also support added amounts for a spouse who needs aid and attendance.

Keep dependent information current. Marriage, divorce, births, children leaving school, or other status changes can affect the payment amount and can create overpayments if VA is not notified when required.

Federal Employment and VR&E

VA's benefit matrix lists a 10-point veteran preference in federal hiring and direct-hire authority among benefits associated with a 100% rating. Actual eligibility for a specific hiring authority depends on federal personnel rules and the job announcement.

Veteran Readiness and Employment (VR&E) is also listed. VR&E is not an automatic cash benefit simply because a veteran is rated 100%; the program makes its own entitlement and rehabilitation-plan decisions. It can support suitable employment, education or training, independent living in appropriate cases, and in some situations self-employment planning.

Commissary, Exchange, MWR and ID Card

VA's matrix lists commissary and exchange privileges and a Uniformed Services ID card at the 100% level. DoD installation access and specific MWR privileges follow DoD rules, so veterans should check current installation and identification requirements before travel.

Benefits That Usually Require Permanent and Total Status

Some of the most valuable family programs are tied to permanence. VA's benefit matrix marks Dependents' Educational Assistance and CHAMPVA as requiring the veteran to be considered permanent. Chapter 35 DEA can help eligible spouses and children with education or training. CHAMPVA can provide health coverage for eligible dependents who are not eligible for TRICARE.

This is why a veteran should not treat “100%” and “100% P&T” as interchangeable search terms. The monthly compensation rate may be the same, but family eligibility can be different.

State Benefits for 100% Disabled Veterans

State-level programs may include property-tax relief, vehicle registration benefits, tuition waivers, state park access, hunting and fishing privileges, or other benefits. These vary dramatically by state and sometimes by county. Some require 100% P&T, some accept a lower rating, and some have residency or income requirements.

Use your state department of veterans services and local tax authority as the source of truth. National “50 benefits you get at 100%” lists often mix federal and state rules together.

100% VA Disability Benefits Checklist

  1. Download your VA benefit summary and decision letter.
  2. Confirm whether your rating is schedular 100%, TDIU, temporary, and/or P&T.
  3. Check VA health-care enrollment and pharmacy benefits.
  4. Apply for VA dental care if eligible.
  5. Review beneficiary travel reimbursement rules.
  6. Make sure all eligible dependents are correctly listed with VA.
  7. Confirm your VA home-loan funding-fee exemption before closing.
  8. Review federal hiring preferences and VR&E if employment goals are relevant.
  9. Check commissary/exchange and ID-card access.
  10. If P&T, review CHAMPVA and Chapter 35 for eligible dependents.
  11. Search your state and county for local disabled-veteran benefits.

100% VA Disability Benefits FAQ

Do 100% disabled veterans get free VA dental care?

VA places veterans with service-connected disabilities rated 100% disabling in Dental Class IV, which may qualify them for any needed dental care. Temporary total ratings do not qualify solely on that basis.

Do I have to be P&T to get CHAMPVA for my family?

CHAMPVA eligibility for a veteran's spouse or dependent child generally requires the veteran to be permanently and totally disabled from a service-connected disability and the dependent to be ineligible for TRICARE.

Does a 100% rating waive the VA home-loan funding fee?

Veterans receiving VA compensation for a service-connected disability generally do not have to pay the VA funding fee. The exemption can apply below 100% as well.

Is 100% schedular the same as TDIU?

No. A schedular 100% rating is reached under VA's rating schedule. TDIU pays at the 100% rate because qualifying service-connected disabilities prevent substantially gainful employment. Different employment rules can apply.
