Space-A Travel for 100% P&T Disabled Veterans: Eligibility, Family Rules and How It Works

Qualifying veterans with a permanent service-connected disability rated as total can use Space-Available military air travel in Category VI. The benefit can be valuable, but it comes with strict geographic rules, lower priority than several other traveler categories, document requirements, and no guarantee that a seat will be available.

Quick answer: Air Mobility Command places veterans with a permanent service-connected disability rated as total in Space-A Category VI. Eligible dependents may travel when accompanied by the veteran sponsor, but non-retiree disabled-veteran travel is limited to specific geographic segments under DoD rules.

Who Qualifies for Space-A as a Disabled Veteran?

Air Mobility Command's current eligibility page includes veterans with a permanent service-connected disability rated as total in Category VI. Category VI is the lowest Space-A priority category and also includes retirees and several other eligible groups.

The wording matters: the eligibility is for a permanent service-connected disability rated as total. A temporary 100% rating is not the same thing. Veterans should carry documentation and the appropriate DoD identification needed by the passenger terminal.

Can Family Members Join a 100% Disabled Veteran on Space-A?

Yes, eligible dependents may travel when accompanied by the qualifying veteran sponsor under the current rule. This does not mean every relative can travel. Passenger terminals will verify dependent eligibility and identification, and dependents generally need to be listed as authorized dependents.

Air Mobility Command terminal guidance specifically states that dependents of qualifying permanently and totally disabled veterans may travel when accompanied by their sponsor. If you are unsure whether a spouse or child has the required ID or documentation, contact the departure terminal before planning the trip.

Where Can a 100% Disabled Veteran Fly Space-A?

For qualifying disabled veterans who are not military retirees, geographic eligibility is more limited than the worldwide Space-A privileges many retirees associate with the program. AMC terminal guidance says travel is authorized in the continental United States or directly between CONUS and Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, and American Samoa. Travel within certain listed non-CONUS U.S. locations is also covered, with specific transit rules for Guam and American Samoa.

This is a major search pitfall. A 100% P&T rating does not automatically give a non-retiree veteran unrestricted Space-A access to Europe or Asia. Always check current DoD Instruction 4515.13 and the passenger terminal before making plans.

How Space-A Travel Actually Works

  1. Verify eligibility and category. Confirm you are Category VI and understand your approved geographic segments.
  2. Find a passenger terminal. Review AMC terminal pages and destinations commonly served.
  3. Sign up. AMC provides automated, manual, and email sign-up options depending on the terminal.
  4. Monitor schedules. Many terminals publish 72-hour flight schedules, but missions can change with little notice.
  5. Mark yourself present. Follow the terminal's check-in instructions for roll call.
  6. Wait for the Space-A call. Seats are offered only after mission requirements and higher-priority eligible travelers are handled.

Documents and Preparation

Travelers should have required DoD identification and, when relevant, passports, visas, and other destination documents. Passenger terminal pages can have location-specific instructions. Dependents must have their own required documents.

Be conservative with baggage and arrival timing. Space-A is not the same as a commercial reservation. Roll-call procedures, baggage limits, mission changes, and terminal services can differ significantly.

Why “Free Military Flights” Is an Oversimplification

Space-A can eliminate the cost of buying a normal airline ticket for an available military flight, but it is a privilege—not guaranteed transportation. AMC explicitly warns travelers to be prepared to buy commercial transportation if schedules change or seats disappear.

Some contracted missions can involve head taxes, segment fees, or other charges. Lodging, rental cars, food, ground transportation, and a backup commercial ticket can also make a “free” trip expensive if plans change. Treat Space-A as flexible travel, not as the foundation for a time-critical event.

Practical Space-A Tips for Disabled Veterans

  • Travel with flexible dates and avoid commitments immediately after your expected return.
  • Keep enough money or credit available for a commercial backup.
  • Check more than one nearby passenger terminal when practical.
  • Save proof of your Space-A signup date and time.
  • Monitor terminal social pages and official 72-hour schedules.
  • Call the terminal when eligibility or document requirements are unclear.
  • Do not assume a route that operated last month will operate again.

Space-A for 100% Disabled Veterans FAQ

Are 100% disabled veterans Category VI?

AMC lists veterans with a permanent service-connected disability rated as total in Category VI.

Can my spouse fly Space-A with me?

Eligible dependents can travel with a qualifying disabled veteran when accompanied by the sponsor, subject to identification and geographic rules.

Can a 100% P&T veteran fly Space-A overseas?

Non-retiree disabled-veteran eligibility is geographically limited. Current DoD guidance permits specified CONUS and U.S. territory/state routes, not unrestricted worldwide travel.

Is a Space-A seat guaranteed after I sign up?

No. Seats depend on mission requirements and availability. AMC tells Space-A travelers to be financially prepared for alternate commercial travel.
